About

Research beyond the obvious.

Hackathon ’26 guides students through a structured research journey: defining a problem, developing a research question, evaluating existing work, analysing evidence, refining arguments, and producing a complete final project.

Beginners are welcome. Participants are guided through a progressive research process, with each stage building on the one before.

  • 01

    Question

    Identify meaningful problems and formulate focused research questions.

  • 02

    Investigate

    Use credible sources, evaluate evidence, and examine competing explanations.

  • 03

    Build

    Develop a coherent research project with defensible reasoning, appropriate references, and proportionate conclusions.

Six research tracks

Choose where to look.

Six interdisciplinary tracks. Each participant selects one in Week 1 and carries it through the full research process.

01

Health & Life Sciences

Explore questions concerning human health, biology, medicine, public health, biotechnology, and life sciences through structured investigation and credible evidence.

02

Technology & AI

Investigate artificial intelligence, emerging technologies, digital systems, technological innovation, and their technical or societal implications.

03

Data Science & Analytics

Explore how data can be collected, interpreted, analysed, and used to investigate questions, identify patterns, and support evidence-based conclusions.

04

Environment & Sustainability

Investigate environmental challenges, climate, conservation, resource use, sustainable development, and possible evidence-based responses.

05

Law, Policy & Global Affairs

Explore legal systems, public policy, international relations, governance, human rights, institutions, and global challenges.

06

Innovation & Entrepreneurship

Investigate business models, social innovation, entrepreneurial ideas, markets, organisational challenges, and the feasibility of proposed solutions.

Seven weeks

From first idea to finished project.

Each stage builds on the previous one. Projects may use different methodologies suited to their subject. Every week lists what to submit.

Week 0111–17 October

Choose a Topic and Define the Problem

Select a research track, identify a problem worth investigating, and turn the initial idea into a focused research question.

Required submission

  • Selected track
  • Proposed research title
  • Focused research question
  • Brief problem explanation
Week 0218–24 October

Plan the Research

Develop a clear research plan explaining what will be investigated, why it matters, what the research will cover, and how the investigation will be approached.

Required submission

  • Research proposal
  • Refined research question
  • Research objectives
  • Scope of the research
  • Significance of the research
  • Preliminary methodology
  • Initial sources
Week 0325–31 October

Understand Existing Research

Examine credible existing research, compare perspectives, and identify gaps, disagreements, or limitations in the available evidence.

Required submission

  • Structured literature review or source review
  • Annotated credible sources
  • Identified gaps, disagreements, or limitations in existing research
Week 041–7 November

Examine the Evidence

Gather relevant evidence, begin analysing it, identify emerging patterns or arguments, and consider the challenges and limitations affecting the investigation.

Required submission

  • Evidence gathered
  • Preliminary analysis
  • Emerging patterns or arguments
  • Limitations or challenges
Week 058–14 November

Build the Complete Project

Bring the research together into a complete first draft with sections appropriate to the subject and a consistent approach to referencing.

Required submission

  • First complete research draft
  • Relevant research sections
  • References
Week 0615–21 November

Improve the Project

Strengthen reasoning, verify claims and citations, address limitations honestly, and ensure that conclusions are supported by the evidence.

Required submission

  • Revised research draft
  • Stronger reasoning
  • Checked claims and citations
  • Limitations
  • Evidence-based conclusion
Week 0722–28 November

Finalise and Submit

Complete a final quality check and submit a coherent, well-supported research project with appropriate references.

Required submission · final

  • Final research project
  • Clear research question
  • Credible evidence
  • Coherent analysis
  • Proportionate conclusions
  • Proper references

FAQ

Common questions.

What is Hackathon ’26?
Hackathon ’26 is an international student research hackathon in which participants develop research projects through a structured, seven-week process.
Is this a coding-only hackathon?
No. It focuses on research, evidence, analysis, reasoning, and project development across six interdisciplinary tracks.
Can beginners participate?
The programme is designed to guide participants through the research process, including defining questions, reviewing sources, analysing evidence, and developing a final project.
What are the six tracks?
  • Health & Life Sciences
  • Technology & AI
  • Data Science & Analytics
  • Environment & Sustainability
  • Law, Policy & Global Affairs
  • Innovation & Entrepreneurship
What must participants submit each week?
Submissions follow the seven-week timeline. The timeline lists the requirements for each stage.
What happens after the final submission?
Final submissions are due during 22–28 November 2026. Judging and results details: To be announced.
What are the prizes?
First place $375, second place $150, third place $50, and three runner-up awards of $25 each, for a total prize pool of $650.
